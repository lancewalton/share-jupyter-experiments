# Swing Trading Trend Linesi

## Definitions

### Trend Lines

Trend lines are straight lines drawn on the price (or ln(price)) chart and they come in two forms:

* Support: these are lines drawn through two LOW candle values at times t=a and t=b, such that the line has a positive gradient, and there is no LOW value at any time between t=a and t=b which is lower than the trend line value at that time
* Resistance: these are lines drawn through two HIGH candle values at times t=a and t=b, such that the line has a negative gradient, and there is no HIGH value at any time between t=a and t=b which is higher than the trend line value at that time

### Rays

Rays are trend lines extended beyond the t=b time all the way to the latest price in the chart.

## General Approach

What we are going to do is put as many rays generated by both types of trend lines on a time series chart in a particular way in order to understand the structure of the line, and then test whether knowing that structure helps to predict price movements, and then determine if there is enough predictive ability to make money.

## Establishing Structure

This is a description of how to establish structure of support. Exactly the same process should be followed for establishing the structure of resistance, but replacing "LOW" for "HIGH" where necessary.

Where we are talking about the time series in this section we are excluding the very latest price point, because that will be the one that gives us the trading signal (or not).

1. Using the full available history of the chart, find the LOWEST price. This is the price at t=a.
2. Find t=b such that we have a line of support, subject to the constraint that there is no LOW value at any time between t=a and the most recent time available in the chart that falls lower than the value of the ray generated by that trend line
3. Now, if possible, repeat step 2 but set a new 'a' value to the last trend line's 'b' value, and find a new 'b' suchn that the conditions specified in step 2 are met. This line of support will have a steeper gradient that the first one.

Repeat steps 2 and 3 until either no more lines of support can be found

Repeat this procedure to find lines of resistance.

## Trading Signals

If it has not been possible to establish at least one line of resistance and one line of support for a given time series, we do not need to continue, because one is needed for the entry, and the other is needed for the exit.

### Entry Signal

I will discuss the entry signal for LONG trades, which means that the entry criteria are determined by the last line of resistance drawn on the chart. This will be the one with the sharpest negative gradient, and with the most recent t=a and t=b. The entry signal for SHORT trades is the same, but using the last line of support.

For the line of resistance to be used as an entry signal, it must have at least three "touch points". It will obviously always have at least two (the t=a and t=b points). But there must be at least one other point somewhere between t=a and the final timepoint in the series that comes "close" to touching the ray generated by the line of support. We have to think about what we mean by "close".

Now, if the latest candle's HIGH broke through the line of resistance, we call this line the "action line", because it has given us the signal to take action. In this case, the action is to go LONG. i.e. we have achieved the entry signal to make a LONG trade.

### Exit Signal

In order to establish the exit condition, we need to use the opposing line to our action line, which we will call the "safety line". So in the case of a LONG trade being indicated by a breached line of resistance, the safety line will be the latest line of support drawn.

The exit signal is given when the price breaches the safety line.

### Stop Loss

When the trade is entered, we should set a stop loss on the lower of (a) the value of the safety line, or (b) a percentage loss above the entry price that represents our appetite for risk. Clearly the latter must take in to account the recent range of the time series, or we will just get thrown out at a loss by intraday swings.

If the price moves in the direction that favours us (i.e. increases for our LONG trade), then we should adjust the stop loss to track the safety line value to lock in profits.

That's the method.

How close to the line is close enough to count it as a touch? Are rapid movements towards the line followed by a rapid movement away a strong touch? Is volatility a consideration? Is the area between the line and the time-series telling us something useful?
2. Do we enter a trade even if the breach is very small, or do we allow a tolerance? Similar question for exiting the trade. If a tolerance, is it the same kind of thinking as that used for the touch point question?
