A quantitative research retrospective · FTSE & UK equities

The Volatility Axis

Four projects asked whether markets can be predicted. They agree on the answer — and on where the value actually is.

Monte-Carlo envelopes · pattern discovery · adaptive filters · risk & regimes  ·  ~40 experiments, 193 series, one thesis

The question

We set out to predict share-price movements — first with Monte-Carlo simulation, then chart patterns, adaptive filters, and volatility regimes. Each project was built to be fooled as little as possible: strict walk-forward evaluation, held-out data, permutation nulls, and a habit of assuming any positive result is a bug until proven otherwise.

What emerged wasn't the strategy we went looking for. It was a sharper understanding of what these series will and won't tell you — and a concrete way to use the half that talks.

The arc · four investigations

Every route led to the same wall

Each project attacked prediction from a different angle. All four hit the same ceiling on direction — and all four found the same open door on volatility.

01 · monte-carlo-experiment

Calibrated envelopes

Bootstrap recent returns into forward price envelopes; judge them by whether reality lands inside, not by drift.

worksFiltered Historical Simulation gives honestly-calibrated tails, validated across 193 series.
failsNaïve resampling never beats a one-line EWMA-vol Gaussian. Direction is pinned to zero — and stays there.
02 · pattern-discovery

Do chart patterns predict?

Discover price-shape patterns directly from data rather than hand-drawing triangles; test them out-of-sample.

worksPatterns carry a statistically real signal (z = 2.4) — and pattern amplitude beats pattern shape.
failsThe signal is economically negligible (IC ≈ 0.006); unsupervised chart patterns are provably meaningless.
03 · adaptive-filter (haf)

Adaptive volatility filters

A hierarchy of self-tuning filters and a mixture of experts, forecasting realised volatility.

worksA fixed signed stack of diverse experts forecasts vol well (skill 0.34) — and upgrades the calibration envelope.
failsEvery adaptive, nested, and self-tuning scheme loses to a single fixed fit. Mean return: unpredictable.
04 · vol-risk-experiment

Risk, regimes & sizing

Use the volatility forecast — not direction — to detect regimes, size positions, and build a portfolio.

worksA causal regime detector predicts forward vol at IC 0.60; the low-vol tilt and the pieces stacked deliver the payoff.
failsMarket-neutral direction bets stay cost-fragile; blind vol-targeting can backfire into rebounds.

What all four agree on

Three through-lines

01

Direction is a one-percent mirage

Across bootstraps, chart patterns, a neural net, a term-structure of drift, and cross-sectional selection, the information coefficient on direction never cleared ~0.01–0.03. Five independent routes, one ceiling — and nothing built on top of it survived transaction costs.

02

Volatility is forecastable — and regime-structured

The same series that hide their direction wear their volatility openly: it clusters, mean-reverts, and switches between calm and turbulent states a causal filter can flag before the moves arrive (IC 0.60). Every piece of durable value in the whole programme sits on this axis.

03

The real split is robust versus overfit — not complex versus simple

Sophistication paid handsomely for volatility (high signal-to-noise) and failed for direction (low). But even on volatility, a six-parameter fit estimated per-instrument overfit and lost, while the same model fit robustly across many series won. Adaptive schemes self-cancelled; equal weights beat fitted weights whenever the signal was thin.

The one thing that generalised

Filtered Historical Simulation

Standardise each return by its own conditional volatility, resample the residuals, and propagate the clustering forward. It was the single method that improved on the trivial baseline and held up out-of-sample across markets.

The payoff

A better way to hold equities — no forecast of direction anywhere in it

The programme's tools are complementary: low-vol selection (cross-sectional), vol-targeting (time-series), and regime-based de-risking each attack risk differently. Stacked, on 122 UK equities (data current to Aug 2026), they roughly double the market's risk-adjusted return and cut its worst drawdown by more than half.

The edge is also crisis-concentrated: it earns its keep in 2008 and 2020, and adds little in calm years. A real result, reported with its asterisks intact.

The one-line version

Direction is a mirage no machinery beats. Volatility is real, forecastable, and regime-structured — and that is exactly what lets you calibrate risk, size it, and build a steadier portfolio.
