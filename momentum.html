A quantitative research programme · the one positive result

The Edge That Held

Direction is a mirage — except for one signal that keeps working: cross-sectional momentum. We found it by accident while trying to kill trend lines, then subjected it to the test that should have debunked it. It survived.

12-1 momentum · UK equities · survivorship-free · net of costs

How we got here

Every attempt to trade on chart geometry failed — support/resistance breakouts, rising-channel timing, buy-the-dip, ride-the-winner, capital rotation. All lost to buy-and-hold, robustly. But one variant looked promising: holding the names that were in strong rising channels. On inspection, that wasn't a trend-line effect at all — it was cross-sectional momentum in disguise. So we isolated it and tested it properly.

Momentum is a ranked-return signal, not a chart pattern: rank stocks by their return over the past 12 months skipping the most recent one (the "12-1"), hold the winners, rebalance monthly. You don't need lines to capture it — and the line-drawing machinery, it turned out, only got in the way.

The test that should have killed it

Survivorship: the alpha survived

Our first momentum backtest used current FTSE constituents — a survivor-only universe that flatters momentum more than almost any factor, because the losers it would have avoided are simply missing. So we bought a genuine survivorship-free universe: 3,237 UK stocks — 1,570 live and 1,667 delisted (Carillion, Thomas Cook, NMC…), full history. The worry was that the edge would evaporate. It didn't.

And the market-neutral book improved (Sharpe 0.25 → 0.65), because shorting the delisted losers — absent from the survivor set — actually pays.

Is it tradeable?

Costs are almost irrelevant

The tilt trades slowly — about 6.3× one-way turnover a year — so realistic frictions barely touch it. Net of a liquidity-tiered spread (15/40/80 bps by size), the long-only tilt earns 10.6% CAGR at Sharpe 0.72, versus the market's 5.65% / 0.42.

The break-even round-trip cost is ~200 bps — an order of magnitude above realistic execution (~15–40 bps). The edge dwarfs the frictions. Monthly rebalancing beats quarterly (Sharpe 0.72 vs 0.63): the turnover saving isn't worth the responsiveness lost, because cost was never the binding constraint.

The cost of admission is drawdown, not cost

The long-only tilt is a high-beta long book (β ≈ 0.84), so its worst months are simply market crashes — −20% in 2008, −17% in the 2020 COVID crash — and its max drawdown is ~−47%. The market-neutral version removes the beta but exposes the momentum crash: a −48% single month in April 2009 and a −64% max drawdown. Real, but a brutal tail to hold or lever.

What we won't claim

Honest caveats

It's a high-beta tilt with real alpha, not market-neutral magic — most of the 10.6% is equity beta; the value-add over that is ~+6%/yr. The survivorship-free universe is messy (bad ticks in delisted micro-caps), so returns are winsorised cross-sectionally — a cleaning judgment the exact numbers depend on, though the liquid top-350 is relatively clean. Results are one market (UK), one construction. And a real edge is not the same as an easy one: you must sit through −47% drawdowns to earn it.

Where it sits in the programme

Direction is a mirage no chart pattern beats. The one exception is momentum — winners keep winning over a year — and it is captured by ranking returns, not by drawing lines.

Across a dozen experiments, every repeatable edge was a risk-premium-shaped thing — sell insurance, bear a crash, collect a modest, honest return. Momentum is the directional member of that family: a real, survivorship-robust, cost-insensitive premium whose price is a fat left tail. It is the programme's standout positive result — and, fittingly, the one idea that owes nothing to the trend lines that led us to it.
