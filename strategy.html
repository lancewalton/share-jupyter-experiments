The Defensive Stack

A risk-first equity strategy that harvests the premia efficiently and controls drawdowns — with no forecast of direction anywhere in it.

Sharpe 0.25 → 0.51 · max drawdown −63% → −38% · market-beta 0.41  ·  net of costs, no look-ahead

The premise

Four projects established two things: the direction of returns is unpredictable at the horizons that matter, and volatility is not — it clusters, mean-reverts, and switches regimes a causal model can flag before the moves land.

A strategy honestly built on that can't time the market. What it can do is hold equities more efficiently: tilt toward the calm names, add the one genuine cross-sectional edge, size by forecast risk, and let the volatility structure — not a direction call — do the work. Each layer below is something the research validated on its own; here they are assembled, and stress-tested together.

The architecture

Three layers that earn their place — and one that's optional

Assembled in order; each row shows what it does and the evidence behind it.

Core — a low-volatility equity tilt

Hold equities weighted toward the lowest-volatility names rather than the cap- or equal-weight market. The workhorse: it captures the equity premium at materially lower risk, long-only and low-turnover.

evidence — same return as market at ⅔ the volatility; drawdown roughly halved

Sleeve — a market-neutral momentum overlay

Add cross-sectional 12-minus-1-month momentum as a separate long/short sleeve. It is the one genuine directional edge — but longer-horizon, and market-neutral, so it diversifies the core's equity beta rather than doubling it.

evidence — IC t = 2.0, net Sharpe 0.34, ≈uncorrelated with the core (−0.03), −0.31 to market

Sizing — vol-target the combined book

Scale total exposure inversely to forecast volatility to hold roughly constant risk — lever up in calm, cut in turbulence. This is where most of the risk-adjusted improvement comes from.

evidence — lifted the book to Sharpe 0.51; on the S&P alone, 0.70 → 0.82

Optional — the systemic regime overlay

De-risking in market-wide crises is, on the corrected data, roughly Sharpe-neutral for the stack (0.51 → 0.53) while cutting drawdown and beta — a defensive layer to add when the mandate prizes stability over a little return. An earlier verdict rejected it outright; that was an artifact of stale data, since fixed.

evidence — maxDD −38% → −31%, beta 0.41 → 0.27, at ann. return 6.2% → 5.2%

What it is

Earns

  • the equity premium, harvested at lower risk and shallower drawdowns
  • a modest, diversifying momentum premium (market-neutral)
  • genuine downside protection — far shallower than the market's worst drawdown

Does not earn

  • anything from predicting direction or timing the market
  • anything from chart patterns or short-horizon signals — mirages
  • a standalone-fund Sharpe — this is efficiency and risk control, not alpha magic

The discipline it comes wrapped in

Rules as much as layers

robust

Estimate every signal pooled / cross-sectional, never fit per-instrument — over-fitting one series was what turned a better model into a worse result, repeatedly.

simple

Combine low-signal-to-noise inputs with equal weights. Optimised weights fit noise; the moment we let a regression choose them, out-of-sample performance fell.

systemic

De-risk on market-wide turbulence, hold through idiosyncratic wobbles — 80% of single stocks rebound from their own turbulence, so per-stock de-risking sells the bottom.

cheap

Favour low turnover. Every edge here is modest and cost-fragile; the core and sleeve are chosen partly because they trade slowly.

honest

Assume a good result is a look-ahead bug until proven otherwise. One inflated this very strategy from 0.61 to 1.14 before it was caught.

Read before believing

Honest caveats

The edge is decaying: split into thirds, the strategy's Sharpe runs +1.49 / +0.29 / −0.16 — most of it earned in the first third, little since. Momentum's contribution has faded (a well-documented post-publication effect) and the crisis-driven benefits are concentrated in 2008 and 2020.

Momentum carries crash risk — its worst single month here was −15%, a five-sigma move, and vol-scaling lifts its Sharpe without taming that tail. And this is one market over one sample (UK equities, 2001–2026); a real deployment needs its own out-of-sample, its own cost model, and its own borrow and capacity assumptions. This is a *better way to hold equities with controlled risk* — not a money machine.
